# Ceremonies

Ceremonies are the regular meetings we have as a product team. Along with the four formal events prescribed by Scrum, we also have workshops that usually take the form of one of the defined UX sessions.

> Block quotes taken from the [Scrum Guide](http://www.scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html#events).

## Retrospective
* **When:** First day of the next Sprint at 10:30am
* **Timebox:** 1 hour

> The Sprint Retrospective is an opportunity for the Scrum Team to inspect itself and create a plan for improvements to be enacted during the next Sprint.

> The purpose of the Sprint Retrospective is to:
> * Inspect how the last Sprint went with regards to people, relationships, process, and tools;
> * Identify and order the major items that went well and potential improvements; and,
> * Create a plan for implementing improvements to the way the Scrum Team does its work.

> By the end of the Sprint Retrospective, the Scrum Team should have identified improvements that it will implement in the next Sprint.

## Sprint Planning
* **When:** First day of the Sprint at 11:30am
* **Timebox:** 30 minutes

> Sprint Planning answers the following:
> * What can be delivered in the Increment resulting from the upcoming Sprint?
> * How will the work needed to deliver the Increment be achieved?

## Daily Standup / Scrum
* **When:** 2pm every day of the Sprint (excluding the first and last days of the sprint)
* **Timebox:** 15 minutes

> The Development Team plans work for the next 24 hours. The Development Team uses the Daily Scrum to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and to inspect how progress is trending toward completing the work in the Sprint Backlog.

We meet in the Glass Conference Room, the last person to arrive starts and we move clockwise from there answering the following questions:
* What have I been working on since the last Standup?
* What am I planning on working on until the next Standup?
* Do I see any impediments that will delay or block me from achieving what I need to achieve?

You may give a traffic light status (green, yellow, red) to give an indication of how you believe your work is going in regards to Sprint completion.

When impediments are identified by team members - the team should collaborate to work out how to remove any blockers.

The Standup should be short and sweet, generally only high level details should be mentioned. Often in-depth discussion with relevant parties will happen soon after the Standup.

Often the Product Owner or Scrum Master will utilise the Daily Standup to ask the team to focus their efforts in a specific area where possible (eg. QA'ing a point release).

## Workshops
* **When:** Tuesdays and Thursdays at 11:00am
* **Timebox:** Varies per session, 2 hours at most

Workshops will take the some form of a UX session, depending on what is required most at the time.

Some sessions will require the whole Product Team, and some will require just the Strike Team assigned to a particular problem space.

See [workshops.md](workshops.md) for a list of the different types of workshops.

## Showcase
* **When:** Last day of Sprint at 2:00pm
* **Timebox:** 2 hours

> A Sprint Review is held at the end of the Sprint to inspect the Increment and adapt the Product Backlog if needed. During the Sprint Review, the Scrum Team and stakeholders collaborate about what was done in the Sprint. Based on that and any changes to the Product Backlog during the Sprint, attendees collaborate on the next things that could be done to optimize value. This is an informal meeting, not a status meeting, and the presentation of the Increment is intended to elicit feedback and foster collaboration.

Showcases are our chance to the whole company up to speed with where we're at with our development, and allow us to collaborate and get feedback on what we've got and where we're going.

Showcases will generally take the following form:
* The Product Owner will opening the meeting with a quick summary what our focus was for the Sprint, what we got "done" and what we didn't get "done".
* The Product Team will showcase their work. Generally the people that did the work will be asked to present their work, but sometimes the Scrum Master will present other people's work.
* Feedback and questions will be asked by the audience at the end of each unit of work shown.
* The Product Owner and Product Team will briefly talk about the plan for the next Sprint, giving the audience a chance to give feedback and ask questions around that.

Work that may be presented:
* Research insights
* Sketches and wireframes
* Prototypes
* Production ready interfaces
* Changes to existing interfaces and functionality

What the Showcase shouldn't include:
* Bug fixes where there is no change to intended functionality - best left for the release notes.
* Minor changes or additions to user interfaces and functionality - it is too hard for the audience to get context with these, best left to release notes and Jira.
* Changes that have no functional impact.

As there will be non-technical people at the Showcase - we should always try to stay away from delving into technical details. These discussions should be parked and happen after the Showcase.
