# Design systems

A design system is a comprehensive and centralized collection of guidelines, components, patterns, and assets that ensures consistency, efficiency, and cohesion in the design and development of digital products.
It serves as a unified reference for designers, developers, and other stakeholders to create and maintain a cohesive user experience across various platforms and devices.
A design system typically includes standards for typography, colour schemes, spacing, iconography, buttons, forms, and other UI elements, as well as principles for interaction and usability.
By promoting reusability and streamlining the design and development process, a well-implemented design system can result in faster delivery of high-quality products and a more coherent brand identity.

## Benefits

Some of the benefits of a design system include:

1. Consistency: A design system ensures a consistent and cohesive visual and interactive experience across all products, platforms, and devices, strengthening your brand identity.

2. Efficiency: Designers and developers can work faster by using pre-defined design patterns, components, and guidelines, reducing redundancy and streamlining workflows.

3. Scalability: As your projects expand, a design system allows for easy scaling by providing standardized elements that can be reused across various applications and services.

4. Collaboration: Designers and developers collaborate more effectively when working with a shared set of guidelines and components, minimizing miscommunications and misunderstandings.

5. Usability: With a user-centered approach, a design system prioritizes usability and consistency, resulting in better user experiences and higher user satisfaction.

6. Maintenance: Updates and changes can be made more efficiently across all projects, ensuring that design decisions remain up-to-date and aligned with evolving user needs.

7. Brand Identity: A design system enforces consistent branding elements, from colors and typography to logo usage, ensuring a strong and recognizable brand presence.

8. Onboarding and Training: New team members can quickly get up to speed by referencing the design system's documentation, guidelines, and components, promoting a smooth onboarding process.

## Moving Forward

As a key principle of our Design System moving forward, we will be adopting the Atomic Design methodology.
Atomic Design is a design methodology created by Brad Frost that helps designers and developers create scalable and maintainable user interfaces.
It breaks down the UI into smaller, reusable components, making it easier to manage and design complex systems.
Atomic Design consists of five distinct stages or levels, each with its own purpose:

<img src={AtomicDesign} alt="Atomic design diagram" className="pantry" />

1. Atoms: At the core of Atomic Design are atoms, which represent the basic building blocks of a design. These are the smallest, indivisible components, such as buttons, input fields, and icons. At this level, you define the most basic styling and functionality.

2. Molecules: Molecules are created by combining atoms. They represent simple, functional components that can perform a specific task, like a search bar or a navigation menu. Molecules are still relatively small and focused on a single function.

3. Organisms: Organisms are larger and more complex components that are made up of molecules and atoms. They can be thought of as sections or modules of a webpage, like a header, footer, or a product card. Organisms combine smaller components to create more significant portions of the user interface.

4. Templates: Templates are page-level structures that provide context for where organisms should appear on a page. They define the layout and arrangement of organisms but don't contain any specific content. Templates are like wireframes or layout guidelines for a page.

5. Pages: Pages represent the final, complete user interfaces. They are built by populating templates with actual content and data. Pages are what end-users interact with and are the culmination of all the lower-level components and structures in the Atomic Design system.

The key principles of Atomic Design include modularity, reusability, and consistency.
By breaking down the design into these discrete levels, designers and developers can work more efficiently, ensure design consistency across a project, and make it easier to maintain and update the user interface as the project evolves.

Overall, Atomic Design is a powerful framework that encourages a systematic approach to UI design and development, making it especially useful for creating scalable and consistent designs in the world of web and application development.
